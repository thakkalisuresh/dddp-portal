Months and popups

The two choices left on the Billing tab, drawn rather than described. Everything here uses the portal’s own type and colour, so what you are judging is the decision and not the styling.

Choice one

Where do past months live?

The tab opens on the month being billed. The question is what happens to July, June and every month before them — and how you reach one when a resident rings about a bill from three months ago.

Choice two

How should popups appear?

Two places need one: the flats that are not being billed, and the residents with no email who have to be told by hand. Both buttons below are live — press them and watch what the page does.

Option A Unfolds in place

7 flats are not being billed and are left out of this count. .

Good
Nothing is covered and nothing is lost. It is the same disclosure idiom as every other fold in the portal, so it needs no new behaviour to learn or to maintain.
Costs
It pushes the page down. On a long step you can open it and not notice, and on a phone it can open below the fold.
Option B Floats over the page

84 of 89 residents were emailed. 5 have no address on file, so .

Escape closes it, so does a click outside.

Good
Impossible to miss, and it holds attention on one task — which suits working through a list of five people to message.
Costs
A second idiom in an app that has twice refused one. It also hides the figures behind it, which is awkward when the list is about them.
Worth knowing before you pick B.

This is a real <dialog>, not window.confirm — so it does not carry the bug that made the notice board’s Withdraw button silently do nothing. The objection to it is consistency, not safety: foldedSection in ui.js says plainly that one screen with its own idiom is a second idiom to maintain.

Nothing here is built. Say A, B or C for the months and A or B for the popups, and the working prototype switches to match.
